Six essays examining the human factors in self-assembling engineering organizations.

[People Are Not Interchangeable]({{< ref "people-are-not-interchangeable.md" >}}) starts from the premise that individual histories, domains of confidence, and motivations shape who participates and how — a reality that self-assembly surfaces rather than creates.

[Initiative Is Unevenly Distributed]({{< ref "initiative-is-unevenly-distributed.md" >}}) examines how voluntary participation can favor people who seek opportunities actively, even when those opportunities are technically visible to everyone.

[Skills Do Not Transfer Equally]({{< ref "skills-do-not-transfer-equally.md" >}}) accounts for domain context, confidence gaps, learning time, and the support costs borne by receiving teams alongside the value of knowledge transfer.

[Psychological Safety]({{< ref "psychological-safety.md" >}}) considers why voluntary movement can stall when asking questions, making mistakes, and contributing early feel professionally risky.

[Visibility Creates Opportunity]({{< ref "visibility-creates-opportunity.md" >}}) argues that outward communication from teams is a prerequisite for participation — people cannot join work they cannot see.

[Movement Is Healthy]({{< ref "movement-is-healthy.md" >}}) addresses both the value of movement across teams and the case for stability. The two themes are paired because neither can be fully understood without the other: the costs of movement are real, and so is the cost of treating stability as the default.
